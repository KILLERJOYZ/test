# Buyer needs and master feature inventory

Prepared 10 November 2026. Scope: Vietnam first; Windows and Android. This is a business-analysis backlog, not an implementation commitment or validated demand forecast.

## 1. Business conclusion

The product should reduce the amount of money, uncertainty, and interruption involved in obtaining useful technology. Expensive devices create several different problems: insufficient upfront cash, fear of choosing incorrectly, pressure to keep working/studying, difficulty comparing promotions, and the consequences of a bad purchase.

Economic growth does not imply that every household can comfortably replace a device. Equally, price-sensitive buyers are not all seeking the cheapest possible product. Some prioritize brand, service, convenience, longevity, or an aspirational purchase. The adviser should expose these preferences without using a budget ceiling as a target to spend.

## 2. Evidence and buying signals

- **Current global smartphone price pressure:** Counterpoint's 3 September 2026 release reports higher existing-model and new-launch prices and describes delayed upgrades, specification tradeoffs, used-device interest, financing, and trade-ins. This is global research, not a Vietnam checkout-price index. [Counterpoint, September 2026](https://counterpointresearch.com/en/insights/global-existing-smartphone-prices-climb-about-15-percent-in-2026)
- **Notebook pressure may affect both prices and specifications:** TrendForce's 8 October 2026 outlook describes cost pressure and potential specification reductions or longer replacement cycles. Its 2027 outlook is a forecast, not an observed future outcome. [TrendForce, October 2026](https://www.trendforce.com/presscenter/news/20261008-13264.html)
- **Vietnam buyers are segmented:** Counterpoint's Q1 2025 Vietnam release describes growth in the mid-price segment alongside premium brand demand and financing. It is historical market context, not a 2026 market-share claim. [Vietnam market research](https://counterpointresearch.com/en/insights/vietnam-smartphone-market-q1-2025)
- **Local qualitative reporting supports several purchase strategies:** Tuoi Tre's July 2026 interviews describe more deliberation, earlier-generation choices, financing, and trade-ins. Reporter and retailer interviews are useful signals, not a representative prevalence estimate. [Tuoi Tre, July 2026](https://news.tuoitre.vn/rising-tech-prices-reshape-buying-habits-in-vietnam-103260727140551146.htm)
- **Actual purchasing contracts matter:** Home Credit publishes separate financing and insurance information; CellphoneS publishes warranty and trade-in entry points. These demonstrate conditions requiring offer-specific verification, not identical terms across lenders or merchants. [Home Credit financing](https://www.homecredit.vn/vay-tra-gop-thiet-bi-dien-tu), [Insurance FAQ](https://www.homecredit.vn/faq/bao-hiem), [CellphoneS warranty](https://cellphones.com.vn/chinh-sach-bao-hanh), [Trade-in](https://cellphones.com.vn/thu-cu-doi-moi)

A food-focused consumer survey was not used to estimate electronics behavior. No national laptop affordability rate, income distribution, financing adoption rate, willingness-to-pay estimate, or quantified feature demand is established here.

**Business inference to test:** useful opportunities exist across planning, comparing, purchasing, and owning. The feature ideas below are hypotheses derived from these signals and the existing project requirements, not features endorsed by the cited researchers.

## 3. Buyer segments and jobs to validate

| Buyer segment | Job the service should help complete | Research question |
|---|---|---|
| Student and parent/payer | Reach a defensible choice for an upcoming course without overspending | Which course requirements and family disagreements delay buying? |
| Worker dependent on one device | Keep earning with minimal disruption and surprise cost | How much does urgency change acceptable compromises? |
| Price-sensitive household | Meet essential tasks while preserving available cash | Is cash price, repayment, condition, or support the binding constraint? |
| Aspirational premium buyer | Obtain desired experience with transparent tradeoffs | What benefit or brand preference justifies extra spending to this person? |
| Existing-device owner | Resolve a specific limitation economically | Which diagnoses and upgrade claims can be supported reliably? |
| Buyer outside a major city | Obtain stock and accessible after-sales support | Are delivery, local service, or travel the main costs? |
| Older/accessibility-focused user | Use a suitable device comfortably with available assistance | Which hands-on checks are missing from current comparisons? |
| Small team or self-employed buyer | Buy compatible equipment with invoices, service and approval evidence | Which procurement steps are too costly for a small purchase? |

These are research segments, not conclusions about every person's age, occupation, income, or abilities.

## 4. Inventory conventions

There are **72 entries: 40 additions or substantial extensions and 32 carried forward** from the prior project research. Features are distinct capabilities; they should not become 72 navigation destinations. Eight starred additions are especially useful candidates for early validation.

- **Earlier:** already discussed; retained for one complete inventory.
- **New/extended:** newly proposed or expanded into a separate capability.
- **Pilot candidate:** eligible for the first scope discussion, not mandatory for the first release.
- **Next / Later:** recommended sequencing based on dependencies, not estimated development hours.
- **Partner-dependent:** requires provider capacity, reliable data/terms, and operational agreements.

The eight starred additions are not a ready-to-ship bundle. Finance comparison, in particular, needs current complete offer inputs and should not hold up a basic recommendation pilot.

## 5. First-release choice

Preserve the required core: editable needs intake, a small exact-variant catalog, deterministic requirements/budget checks, meaningful comparisons, sourced explanations, reliable local offer totals, and the two platform adapters.

Validate a few additions around that core:
- Previous-generation discovery and configuration downgrade detection.
- A shareable decision brief/family review flow.
- A device inspection checklist and private purchase-record/deadline reminders.
- Installment comparison when current complete data supports a responsible comparison.

Defer a marketplace, app-issued loans, custody of family contributions, autonomous checkout, guaranteed buyback values, unsupported price forecasts, and subscriptions/rental operations. Referrals or comparison tools can be introduced before the app takes on those operational responsibilities.

## 6. Shared capabilities behind the features

Avoid separate disconnected engines. Build shared modules:
1. Profile and household/team permissions.
2. Exact product/variant and support catalog.
3. Workload/compatibility rules and scenario evaluator.
4. Offer, promotion, payment-schedule and basket-total calculations.
5. Evidence, confidence, freshness and correction service.
6. Saved searches, deadlines and notification policies.
7. Private documents, quotations and approved exports.
8. Reusable WinUI/Expressive component contracts and platform adapters.

For installment tools, display offer inputs, total payable and missing information; distinguish optional extras. User-entered cash-flow scenarios are not creditworthiness decisions, approval promises, or a universal affordability rule. Do not require bank credentials to explore a device budget.

For receipt/quotation imports, keep sensitive identifiers private, permit redaction/deletion, and separate personal documents from any AI-processing consent. Verification checklists do not prove authenticity, lawful ownership, or absence of hidden damage.

## 7. Validation metrics and commercial implications

| Feature area | Useful outcome metric | Counter-metric |
|---|---|---|
| Price and financing | Correct complete-total comparisons; user understanding | Missing mandatory costs or misleading repayment framing |
| Older-model/configuration search | Suitable lower-cost alternatives accepted | Support gaps or hidden downgrades missed |
| Used-device guidance | Material issues correctly identified in reviewed cases | Unsupported assurances or false accusations |
| Household/shareable briefs | Fewer unresolved decisions and clearer agreement | Exposure of private profile/financial information |
| After-purchase tools | Accurate records and timely useful reminders | Wrong policy deadlines or excessive notifications |
| Workload/PC advice | Requirements satisfied in expert-reviewed tasks | Hard-constraint violations and unknown compatibility passed |
| Business sustainability | Cost per useful decision; trusted repeat use | Commission-driven rank changes and costly source maintenance |

Measure verified savings against comparable offers for the same need and terms, not against inflated list prices. Count no-purchase and lower-cost outcomes as successes when appropriate. Report sample sizes, errors and data coverage.

Retain a useful free basic adviser and offer checks. Paid opportunities may include optional human review, advanced household/team workflows and administrative procurement features, subject to willingness-to-pay research. Paid placements should remain separate from organic suitability. Do not require payment to discover a material qualification in the app's own recommendation.

## 8. Next research cycle

Use interviews and task observation with students/payers, workers, used-device buyers and small teams. Recruit people with recent actual purchases or current purchase deadlines. Ask for consented anonymized examples of quotations, payment schedules and confusing warranty terms.

Test individual feature prototypes with fixed catalog/offer snapshots before building an integrated service. Include tasks in which the right result is to wait, buy a less expensive device, repair, or reject an uncertain offer. Have technical/price accuracy reviewed independently of user preference. Prioritize the features that improve decisions and can be maintained with available data rights and operating capacity.
